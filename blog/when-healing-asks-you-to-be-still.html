When healing asks you to be still

By Ashley RoseNovember 5, 20265 min read

After 2020, my life came to a stop. For three years I couldn’t walk. I want to share a little of what that season taught me, because many women are living their own version of it: a diagnosis, a loss, a burnout, a heartbreak that makes everything stand still.

Stillness is not the same as stuck

At first, stillness felt like punishment. I measured my days by what I couldn’t do. Slowly I began to notice that stillness was also giving me something: time to listen. To my body, to my heart, and to Heaven.

Stillness gave me what busyness never had: time to listen.

Patterns appeared in the quiet

In that quiet, the ideas that became the JOY Connection Map started to form. I noticed which days felt lighter and why. They were the days I had prayed, the days someone had truly seen me, and the days I had been gentle with myself. Heaven, others and you.

Healing is not a straight line

There were setbacks. There were weeks when nothing seemed to change. What carried me was small, repeatable practices, and people who reminded me who I was when I forgot. That is still the heart of how I work with women today.

If you are in your own still season

  • Let rest be part of the work, not a failure to work.
  • Name one person you can be honest with this week.
  • Ask Heaven for strength for today, not for the whole road.
  • Notice one small thing that brings you joy, and do it again.

You were made to fly. Sometimes the first step is learning to be still. If you’d like company on the way, I’d love to hear from you.
